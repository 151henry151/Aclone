## Planned — Odd Jobs Office pay and NPC labour behaviour

- **Problem:** a 15-second public labour shift pays **1d** (`labourReward` in `src/shared/public-labour.ts` returns a hard-coded 100 hundredths), cut from 45d in 0.64.0 to stop NPCs treating the Odd Jobs Office as an unlimited career. At 1d it is never worth a player's time: a Harbour loaf and water cost about 150d, so a meal is 150 shifts (over 37 minutes of clicking). The earlier 15d figure was already low. Means-tested relief (`RELIEF_PAY`, 150d once per 30 minutes below 50d combined cash and savings) remains the real safety net.
- **Pay:** raise the ordinary shift to a rate that is worth doing when nothing better is available but clearly worse than any real job. Calibrate against the wage scale rather than picking a number: business wages in `data/buildings.json` are 8d, 12d and 22d per batch, and the Odd Jobs Office's own listed wage is 22d; a target in the range of **10–20d per 15-second shift** (40–80d per minute of continuous work) should be checked against gathering, hauling and the cheapest shop prices so a new player can buy their first meal in a few minutes of labour without labour out-earning a farmhand. Keep the relief payment and cooldown. Make the shift rate a world setting (Editor → Rules, with the Puddlewick default applied by a one-time versioned migration like `boatDeathRulesVersion`) so other worlds can tune it; update the NPC `labour` briefing text in `src/server/npc/strategy.ts`, PLAYING.md, ECONOMY.md and the first-steps hints, which all quote 1d.
- **NPC behaviour:** residents already fall back to labour only when a meal is unaffordable (`src/server/npc/care.ts`) or when cash and savings are below their household reserve and no hauling pays (`src/server/npc/routines.ts`). With a higher rate, prevent the loop from becoming a permanent livelihood by rule rather than by starving the pay: a diminishing return within a session (for example full pay for the first few consecutive shifts, then a declining fraction that resets after other work or a real break), a cap on labour income per game day per resident, and a stronger preference in the chooser for any qualified job, gathering with an owned implement or a supply intent over another shift once the resident is out of immediate need. Public labour must stay an emergency bridge in the NPC briefing and in the offline-evaluation heuristic chooser.
- **Players:** the same diminishing-return and daily-cap rules apply to players so the office cannot be farmed by a script, and the shift button shows the current payment and why it is reduced.
- **Tests:** new shift pay and relief unchanged; diminishing return and daily cap for players and NPCs; the NPC chooser leaves labour for a vacancy or hauling when one exists and returns to labour only in need; migration applies once to Puddlewick and not to worlds that set their own rate; documentation strings match the setting.

Add a setting **Eat and drink automatically from inventory while online** (default off) alongside the existing offline behaviour (kept on where it is on today); in public Puddlewick, turn automatic inventory consumption **off while a player is online** and keep it on while offline, via a one-time versioned migration that does not overwrite later owner edits. ## Planned — Solid trees, boulders and vehicles

- **Problem:** much of the landscape can be driven straight through. Movement (`move` in `src/shared/simulation.ts`) only tests cliffs, inland water, buildings, parked trailers, creator-placed barriers and landscape fences; it never tests trees, boulders, scenery rocks, resource-ground stones or other players. Trees and rocks are generated **on the client only** (`src/client/countryside.ts` seeds each streamed chunk and `src/client/scenery.ts` scatters the village surroundings, with `countryside` counts that differ between low and normal quality), so the server does not know where a tree is and cannot stop anyone at it. Players and their vehicles likewise pass through each other.
- **Shared, deterministic obstacles:** move tree and boulder placement into a shared module that both server and client call: a per-chunk seeded generator producing `{x, z, kind, radius, height}` for every solid object, independent of render quality (quality may skip drawing some, never place different ones), honouring the same exclusions as today (roads, buildings and construction, fields, pastures, gathering grounds, airfields, spaceport fence, water). Cache per chunk with a spatial hash so `move()` can query obstacles near a swept segment in constant time, invalidate when buildings or roads change, and include coastal rocks (`src/client/coastal-rocks.ts`), scenery rocks and the boulders at stone gathering grounds. Trunks block with a small radius so a tractor fits between most trees in open woodland but not through a dense northern forest; crowns do not block. Creator scatter already has a `solid` flag; keep that path and make the default procedural scenery solid in the same way.
- **Movement response:** collision with a tree or boulder stops the vehicle (speed to zero, as the cliff and inland-water branches do) with no damage, condition loss or fuel penalty; walking players slide around round obstacles rather than sticking. Keep `allowEscape` behaviour so anyone who loads inside a newly solid tree can drive out. Boats are unaffected except for coastal rocks they already avoid via cliffs.
- **Vehicle-to-vehicle collision:** treat every other player on land as an oriented footprint (the vehicle's `length` × `width`, or the walking `collisionRadius`) and test the swept segment against it. **No damage** to either party. Which vehicle stops depends on the geometry of the impact: a vehicle that drives into a stationary or slower one stops itself and the other is unaffected; a head-on closing between two moving vehicles stops both; a glancing contact below a shallow angle slides along the other hull and continues at reduced speed. There is no push, shove or bounce, so a parked tractor cannot be rammed off a road and a stationary player is never moved by someone else's input. Walking players cannot pass through a tractor but can step around it, and a tractor cannot drive through a pedestrian (it stops short), which also stops the existing silent overlap of two riders on one spot.
- **Prediction and fairness:** the server owns the outcome. Client prediction tests against the latest snapshot positions of other players, accepts small corrections on contact, and never predicts another player's stop. Decide what counts as an obstacle when its owner is offline or at home: an offline player's vehicle persisting at its last position becomes a permanent obstacle, so either hide it from collision after sign-off or apply the parked-trailer rules. Add an anti-griefing rule for vehicles left across a narrow route (gates, bridges, jetties, the arrival area): the existing arrival-area and town-centre clearances should refuse stopping there, and a vehicle stationary with its driver offline for a period should not block.
- **Scope and performance:** the obstacle query must stay cheap at the enlarged island's density (hundreds of trees per chunk, several moving players); test the per-tick cost with `scripts/island-performance.ts`. Road growth, NPC ground navigation and construction previews should consult the same obstacle data so NPCs do not path through trunks and new roads are not grown through a solid boulder. Snow accumulation already treats trees as `snowObstacle`; reuse the same objects.
- **Tests:** identical obstacle sets for the same chunk at both quality levels and across server/client; a tractor stopped by a trunk and a boulder without condition loss; walking slide-around; escape from inside an obstacle; rear-end, head-on and glancing vehicle contacts with the correct vehicle(s) stopping and no position change for the struck party; pedestrian versus tractor; offline-owner rule; no regression in the northern-island movement tests (fords, deep reaches) or road growth; frame and tick budgets unchanged.

## Planned — River crossings, bridges and highway haulage

### Persistent roads and distinct river-crossing rules

- Established dirt and paved roads persist when a new route opens. Adding a shortcut, bridge or intercity highway must not erase an older usable route. Only explicit changes such as rerouting around a building/field/obstruction, demolition or an owner-authorized edit may retire the affected segment. Preserve old ford roads when a bridge is built.
- When a dirt-road project connects towns on opposite sides of a river, search along the river for the nearest **usable ford**, then snake the road to that crossing and onward to the destination. Evaluate an actual connected route, not just proximity to a river centreline: the whole crossing and approaches must meet ground-vehicle depth, width, gradient and bank-clearance limits. Prefer the nearest feasible ford that makes geographic sense for the journey; never grow a dirt road across an impassably deep reach.
- Make the ford search work at the enlarged island's scale, including a town founded far across a river. Use a river/ford graph or staged route search instead of a single enormous fine-grid search. Cache surveys and invalidate them when terrain, water rules, bridges or obstructions change.
- Paved roads have a different policy: they may lead directly toward a river and stop safely at its bank while awaiting a bridge. They **must not detour to a ford or pave an existing ford crossing** merely to claim a continuous paved connection.
- When a usable bridge exists, paved-road routing can select it and connect across it, taking reasonable approach detours. The paved network's connectivity must count bridges but exclude dirt fords, even where the ordinary vehicle road network is already connected.
- Where unfinished paved approaches stop on opposite banks, allow dirt feeder roads to grow from their ends toward a usable ford, preserving an interim cross-river route. A later bridge can complete the paved route while those older dirt feeders remain.
- Integrate these rules with the separate city-paving backlog: sustained city qualification, slow centre-outward paving toward dense urban areas, dirt-only rural/farm access lanes, boundary-first expansion, and intercity destinations only. Paved crossings do not grant smaller towns city status or trigger paving toward them.

Costs should reflect span length and design; avoid a trivial cheap crossing of a broad navigable river.
- Survey and preview a valid bank-to-bank span, stable approaches, foundations, safe deck grade and sufficient clearance above the river. Account for water levels and retain boat passage where the river is navigable; supports, vehicle collision and rendered decks must agree.

## Planned — Earthmoving machines and a building slope limit

Two connected pieces: a stricter, size-aware slope rule for construction, and late-game bulldozers and backhoes that can cut and fill the ground to make level pads, used by their owners or hired out to other players. The slope rule gives earthmoving a purpose; earthmoving gives the slope rule an answer.

### What exists today

- `foundationProfile` in `src/shared/construction-site.ts` samples a footprint every two metres and `constructionSiteReason` refuses any site whose rise exceeds **2 m**, regardless of building size ("Ground rises more than 2 metres across this footprint"). A 4 m shed is allowed on a 45% slope while a 30 m hall is refused on a 6% one. Completed buildings store `foundationY` and keep their datum.
- Terrain is a function, not a grid: `terrainHeight` in `src/shared/terrain.ts` composes the village, countryside, northern island, creator heightmap, world-owner **terrain stamps** (`w.terrain`, additive cones with radius 1–100 m and height ±30 m, at most 256 per world, placed by the `terrain` action, owner only), coastal bluffs, airfield and spaceport grading, and the seabed. Collision, water, fords, roads, construction, NPC navigation, the map worker and the rendered chunks all read it, so any earthwork that changes `terrainHeight` propagates everywhere by construction. `airfieldHeight` and `spaceportGround` already show the shape wanted: a flat pad with smooth shoulders blended into the natural ground.
- Spoil goods already exist (`topsoil`, `gravel`, `stone`, `sand`), as does the quarry tipping trailer (1,000 weight of those goods) and the `excavator` gathering skill name, which this work must not collide with.

### Slope limit for construction

- Replace the flat 2 m rule with a **grade** rule: allowed rise scales with the footprint span, for example at most ~12% grade along either footprint axis (rise ÷ span) plus a small absolute allowance so tiny buildings are not stricter than today, with an absolute ceiling (the current 2 m) for anything. Keep it lenient: ordinary village lots, gentle hillsides and river terraces remain buildable without machinery; steep valley sides, ridge noses and mountain slopes are not. Fields and pasture parcels get their own, slightly more lenient grade check; roads keep their existing cost-based grade handling.
- The refusal message quantifies the problem and the remedy: "Ground rises 3.4 m (17%) across this footprint; level it to within 1.8 m with earthworks, or choose a flatter site". The build-menu site plan shades the footprint by how far each sample is above or below the achievable pad level, so a player can see where cut and fill are needed before hiring a machine.
- Existing buildings are never re-validated; the new rule applies to new placements, expansions and relocations only. Make the grade thresholds world rules (Editor → Rules → Construction) with Puddlewick defaults, and give the world owner a bypass since they can already stamp terrain.

### Earthworks data model

- Add `w.earthworks`: a list of **pads**, each an oriented rectangle (or a short polygon) with a target level, a shoulder width and maximum shoulder grade, an owner, a creation time, a progress fraction and a surface finish (bare earth → gravel → grass as it weathers). `terrainHeight` applies pads after stamps and before bluffs/airfield grading, blending natural ground to the target level across the shoulder exactly as `spaceportGround` does, scaled by progress so a half-finished pad is visibly half dug. Cache pads in a spatial grid; `terrainHeight` is hot and must not scan every pad for every sample.
- Limits: a maximum pad size (for example 60 × 60 m) and shoulder, a maximum cut or fill depth (for example 6 m), a per-world pad count and total area, and a rule that pads may not overlap buildings, roads, fields, pastures, gathering grounds, town centres and arrival areas, no-build zones, the airfield, the spaceport reservation, coastal bluffs, inland water and its banks, or another player's unfinished pad. Pads may overlap and supersede earlier **completed** pads by the same owner (re-grading). Rivers and ponds cannot be filled or diverted in this slice.
- Pads persist in snapshots and portable designs, survive restarts, and are included in the terrain revision used by the map worker and streamed chunks so both rebuild the affected area. Trees and boulders inside a pad and its shoulder are removed by the shared obstacle generator (see the solid scenery item) once progress passes the point where the ground under them has changed; the gathering-ground and woodland exclusions already handle similar cases.

### Machines

- Two vehicle definitions, both **manufactured only at the automotive factory** (see the haulage programme) and therefore unavailable until that industry exists; prices on the order of a tractor-trailer, with heavy fuel burn:
  - **Bulldozer** — tracked, slow (around 8 km/h, much slower off any surface than a tractor), wide front blade, large volume per pass, best for pushing cut material across a pad into fill. Cannot tow. Modelled in detail: tracks that animate and leave the existing tyre-track system's marks as plates, blade lift, cab with human-scale operator, lights, exhaust stack.
  - **Backhoe / excavator** — wheeled, faster on roads, front loader bucket and rear digging arm; smaller volume per cycle but precise, can load its spoil into an adjacent tipping trailer and can dig below the pad for trenches in a later slice. Animated boom, stick, bucket and stabiliser legs when working.
- Operating either requires a new **plant operator** qualification learned at school (distinct from the existing `excavator` gathering skill and the `driver` licence), counted against the skill limit like other licences, and required regardless of the world's `vehicleLicences` toggle in Puddlewick; driving one onto a road without the skill is refused the same way as other licence checks.

### How the work is done

1. **Survey.** The owner (or a client, see contracting) opens **Earthworks** from the machine's panel or the build menu, draws a pad on the site plan (size, rotation, target level clamped between the footprint's lowest and highest natural points plus the depth limits), and sees the computed **cut volume**, **fill volume**, net spoil or shortfall, shoulder extent, estimated passes and fuel, and any placement refusal. Nothing is charged until the pad is confirmed; confirming reserves the area (no construction there until the pad completes or is abandoned).
2. **Working passes.** The operator drives the machine inside the pad with the blade or bucket engaged (a key or button, like trawling or the flashlight). Progress accrues only while the machine is moving slowly within the pad with the implement down; each metre travelled moves a volume set by the machine, the material (topsoil is cheap to move, rock-bearing upland costs several times more, read from the local resource province) and the operator's condition/fuel. Progress is a fraction of total volume so the ground morphs gradually and visibly. The server owns progress; the client previews the blade lowering and dust.
3. **Material balance.** Cut material first supplies fill within the same pad. Net surplus goes into the machine's spoil hopper (bulldozer: a berm pushed to the pad edge represented as a small stamp; backhoe: real goods in a bucket) and from there into a tipping trailer or a spoil heap, producing saleable `topsoil`/`gravel`/`stone` at a conversion rate below quarrying so earthworks is not a better mine than a quarry. Net shortfall must be delivered as `gravel` or `topsoil` to the pad before the last fill passes can complete, using the ordinary trailer and inventory transfer flows; the panel shows what remains to be delivered.
4. **Completion and weathering.** When progress reaches 1 the pad is level to within a tolerance (for example ±0.15 m) and the reservation turns into a buildable surface that passes the grade rule. The surface starts as bare earth, becomes gravel if gravel was spread, and greens over game days. Abandoning a pad part way leaves the partial earthwork in place as scarred ground; it can be resumed by any machine with the owner's consent.
5. **Shoulders and neighbours.** Shoulders are computed from the final level and the maximum shoulder grade so a 4 m cut into a hillside produces a sloped bank, never a vertical wall; if the shoulder would intrude on a neighbour's building, road or field, the pad is refused or shrunk at survey time. Roads crossing a pad's shoulder are re-graded by the ordinary road rules on their next maintenance pass.

### Contracting and permissions

- A machine owner can **take contracts**: a client surveys and confirms a pad on open land (or on their own property clearance), posts it with an offered fee, and any plant operator can accept. Reuse the tug rescue and charter boat **escrow** flow: the fee is held when the operator accepts, released on completion, refunded if the operator abandons, and split proportionally if a different operator finishes. Materials for net fill are the client's responsibility unless the contract says otherwise. Contracts appear in the parish directory and the World list next to supply orders so remote towns can find a dozer.
- Permission: on open land anyone may pad; inside a town boundary the town's construction rules and taxes apply (towns may disable earthworks for guests like road building); on another player's premises the owner must confirm. NPC residents may post contracts when a desired business site fails the grade rule and they can afford the fee, which gives the machine a steady market without automation of the work itself.

### Implementation order and acceptance checks

1. Replace the 2 m rule with the grade rule and the quantified message plus site-plan shading; prove small, medium and large footprints on 5%, 12% and 25% slopes are accepted or refused as intended and that no existing building is affected.
2. Add `w.earthworks`, the pad shape in `terrainHeight` with spatial caching, persistence, export/import and chunk/map invalidation; prove a completed pad passes the grade rule, water and fords are untouched, and `scripts/island-performance.ts` shows no measurable `terrainHeight` regression with 200 pads.
3. Add the plant operator skill and the two machines with purchase through the automotive factory, driving, fuel, detailed models and animations; prove licence enforcement and that the dozer is slow off-road.
4. Add survey, passes, material balance, spoil goods, delivery of fill, completion tolerance and weathering; prove cut-only, fill-only and balanced pads, spoil conversion below quarry yield, and resumption after abandonment and restart.
5. Add contracts with escrow, permissions and NPC posting; prove payment, refund and hand-over cases.
6. Browser and preview coverage: a pad surveyed on a northern hillside, dug in passes, built on; shoulders meeting a road; trees removed from the pad; mobile and controller controls for the implements.

The automotive factory, tractor-trailers and solid scenery items are prerequisites or close relatives; schedule this after the factory exists so the machines are genuinely late-game.

## Planned — Player-built airstrips and commercial aviation

The purpose is intercity air travel once towns exist far across the expanded island: a player builds an airstrip near a distant settlement, buys passenger or cargo aircraft, and runs flights between airstrips for a fare or a freight margin. Today there is exactly one public airfield (`w.airfield`, a single north/south reservation with one hangar, `src/shared/airfield.ts`) and one aircraft type, the biplane (slot 2, 90 weight hold).

### Buildable airstrip

- Add an **airstrip** building kind, placed through the ordinary build menu with the usual five architectural variants, finishes, rotation and site plan. Its footprint is a graded runway strip plus an apron with a small hangar/office crib; the runway should be long enough for every planned aircraft's takeoff and landing run with margin (the biplane needs roughly 150–200 m; larger aircraft will need more, so size the strip to the heaviest planned type, likely 400–600 m by 30 m, and decide whether two strip lengths are offered).
- Make it **extremely expensive**, well beyond a bridge (about 800s target) and the boathouse (18s): a starting target on the order of **2,000–3,000s** plus very large material quantities drawn from goods that already exist — gravel and concrete for the strip base and apron, steel and wood or logs for the hangar, blocks for the office — with final figures calculated against the economy so it is a late-game community investment rather than something one player buys early. Support material contribution by several players and construction helpers like other large sites, with persistent unfinished progress and the existing cancellation refund rules.
- Siting rules: the whole rotated strip must be dry ground outside protected areas, other premises, fields, pastures, roads and inland water, with limited slope end to end and across; the construction preview must show the graded profile and refuse sites that need more than a bounded cut/fill. Grading should reuse the `airfieldHeight` approach so the runway is flat and terrain, collision, trees and rendering agree; trees inside the strip and an approach cone at each end are cleared, and later construction inside the approach cones is refused.
- Generalise the single `w.airfield` reservation into a list of airfields with arbitrary rotation and an owner, and make `onAirfield`, `onRunway`, `airfieldHeight`, road routing, farm/pasture placement, resource placement and the map honour every entry. The public Puddlewick airfield migrates into the list unchanged (same hangar ID, apron and grade). Add `hasAircraftHangar`-style checks per airstrip so boarding, commissioning and parking work at any strip, and keep the saved-aircraft model (fleet per player, active airframe, spare airframes) rather than inventing a second one.
- Define ownership and access before implementation: whether a private airstrip is open to all pilots, landing fees set by the owner, parking/hangarage of other players' aircraft, and what happens to parked aircraft when the owner demolishes or sells the strip. The parish map shows airstrips with an icon and name, and the parish directory lists them with distance so pilots can plan a route.
- Runway lighting and a windsock are rendered detail; lighting matters for night flights and should be visible from the air at distance like the lighthouses.

### Additional aircraft types

- Add several new aircraft definitions in `data/vehicles.json` sharing `src/shared/flight.ts` dynamics with per-type mass, wing area, drag, thrust, stall speed, fuel burn and takeoff/landing distances, each modelled in detail with cockpit/cabin interiors at human scale. A starting set: a **light passenger aircraft** (4–6 passengers, modest cabin baggage), a **larger passenger aircraft** (8–12 passengers, needs the long strip), a **light utility/cargo aircraft** (a few hundred weight), and a **cargo aircraft** (roughly 1,000–1,500 weight, comparable to one or two tipping trailers, needs the long strip). Prices should scale sharply with capacity; the cargo aircraft and large passenger aircraft are late-game purchases.
- Cargo capacity is a hard **weight** limit, not a slot count, using the existing per-item weights, so heavy goods (fish, stone, ore) fill a hold fast while light goods pack well. Loaded weight changes performance: longer ground run, slower climb, higher stall speed, more fuel; an overloaded aircraft cannot be loaded, not merely warned. Fuel is bought from a hangar/airstrip in the same way as boats buy from a boatyard, and a strip's owner may stock fuel for sale.
- Loading and unloading happen only parked on an apron and use the ordinary carry/transfer flows; decide whether aircraft can trade directly with a shop within range (like trailers) or only through the pilot's inventory. Define parking: an aircraft shut down on an apron stays visible and persists like a moored boat; an aircraft abandoned on open ground outside any strip is either refused or recovered to the nearest strip for a fee.
- Keep existing biplane behaviour, the Boatmaster/pilot licence rules, crash destruction of the active airframe and the authoritative crash notice unchanged; the new types extend the same code paths and tests.

### Passenger flights as a business

- A pilot with a passenger aircraft offers **flights**: announce a destination airstrip and fare, accept boarding passengers at an airstrip, fly, and settle on arrival. Reuse the charter boat's invitation, ticket and fare-escrow model (`src/shared/charter*.ts`) so money is held when the passenger boards and paid to the pilot when the passenger disembarks at a strip, with refunds if the pilot cancels or the flight never leaves. Walk-up boarding at a strip and a parish directory listing of flights on offer both need designing; avoid requiring a passenger to be in chat with the pilot.
- Passengers ride in a cabin seat with a window view, cannot control the aircraft, and may leave only on the ground at a strip (or by the existing rules for leaving a vehicle on the ground elsewhere; never in flight). A passenger may carry limited baggage: a small weight allowance per seat (on the order of 20–30 weight, well below a trailer and a fraction of the hold), enforced at boarding against what the passenger is carrying; excess must be left behind or shipped as cargo.
- A **crash kills everyone aboard**. Each passenger dies under the ordinary death rules — inventory/property/boat consequences as the world configures them — and respawns where they normally would (the parish arrival area or home), with the same notice style as the pilot's crash notice naming the flight and pilot. Escrowed fares for a crashed flight are refunded, not paid. Pilot liability (reputation, fines) is a design question to settle before implementation, not an assumption.
- Mixed loads: a passenger aircraft may carry small cargo in a hold, and a cargo aircraft may carry a very small number of passengers only if its definition says so; the weight limit is shared.
- NPC residents should eventually be able to buy flights to move between towns when that makes economic sense for them, using the same tickets; this is a later slice.

### Air freight economics

- Cargo flights are meant to profit from **price differences between developing towns** (the existing per-town business prices and local shortages from the northern resource provinces). Fuel burn per kilometre, aircraft price, maintenance and the hard weight cap set the margin; calibrate so a full cargo aircraft on a long route beats a trailer only where roads are poor or distances are long, and never beats a cargo boat or truck on bulk goods per unit cost. Document the expected margins per route length in HAULAGE.md once tuned.
- Air freight interacts with spoilage and perishables (fish), supply intents and parish supply orders; confirm deliveries by air count like any other delivery.

Price in the same range (a starting target of **55–60s**), and a capacity in the same range as the liquid tanker in weight units of Fuel (each Fuel is 1 weight and adds 8 tank units), so a full tanker holds several hundred Fuel; confirm the exact figure against the existing tank sizes (trawler 3,200, tug 512, charter 192, motorboat 24) so one tanker can refuel a large vessel without being an unlimited reserve. `trailerAccepts` admits only `fuel`; the liquid tanker continues to refuse it.

Cash goes to the owner (or the owner's bank when offline), sales tax follows the ordinary rules for the town the tanker is in, and the transaction is logged in the ledger like a shop sale. - Fire and safety realism is out of scope; the tanker does not explode when hit. Refuelling a boat from a tanker still requires the boat to be alongside land within range, as for carried Fuel.
- Tests: `trailerAccepts` fuel-only rule and liquid tanker unchanged; filling from a refinery shop; direct vehicle refuel from a tanker with correct charge, tank cap and owner payment; inventory purchase and tax; free owner use; offline owner payment to bank; parked-trailer persistence of price and load across restart and export; the HAULAGE.md trailer list and the garage purchase panel updated.

## Planned — CB radios

- Add **CB radio** to vehicles: boats, tractors, aircraft, the future tractor-trailers and cargo boat, with an optional base station in buildings as a later slice. The radio is a separate text channel from the world-wide public chat, modelled on real 27 MHz citizens' band behaviour rather than as a chat room.
- Realistic simulation: **40 numbered channels**, one selected per radio, with channel 9 reserved for emergencies and channel 19 as the conventional road/haulage channel. **Push-to-talk**: the sender keys the microphone (a held key or button, with a typed message delivered as a transmission whose on-air duration scales with its length at a speaking rate), and **only one transmitter at a time can be received on a channel within range**. If two stations key simultaneously, listeners in range of both hear a garbled collision ("doubling"); a station that keys while another transmission is already being received is told the channel is busy and does not get through. Squelch hides weak signals; a volume/squelch control and a channel selector live in the vehicle dashboard, with keyboard, touch and controller bindings.
- Propagation: line-of-sight-ish range limited by distance and terrain, not world-wide. Starting targets: a few kilometres between vehicles on the ground, further from a hilltop or an aircraft, further still over water, with the mountain and ridges blocking; signal strength degrades text legibility progressively (dropped words, then static) rather than cutting off at a hard radius. Compute reception per listener on the server from sender/receiver positions and a cheap terrain occlusion sample; the island at ten times its area is about 30 km across, so cross-island contact should require relays or aircraft, which gives aviation and hilltop stations a role.
- Radio messages are not stored in the chat log as public chat; show a radio pane with the active channel, received traffic with the sender's call sign (player name or an owner-set handle), signal strength and who is currently keyed. AI residents do not answer CB traffic initially. Decide whether radio text is moderated by the same filters and `chatLocked` as public chat (it should be).
- Emergency use: channel 9 calls from a stranded or sinking vessel can be surfaced to nearby boats as a rescue prompt, tying into the existing lifeboat and fuel-rescue flows without adding automation.
- Tests: channel isolation, single-transmitter rule and collision behaviour, keyed duration from message length, range/terrain attenuation, aircraft and hilltop reach, moderation parity with chat, dashboard controls on each platform, and persistence of a radio's selected channel and handle with the vehicle.

## Planned — Canoes

- Add a **canoe**: the smallest watercraft, paddled (no fuel), very low speed, a hold on the order of **10–15 weight**, one occupant. It is an owned item that exists in one of several states: **carried** (upside down on the player's head), **set down** on land anywhere, **launched** on any water, **moored** at a bank, or **stored** in the owner's home. Persist the state and position in the world like `mooredBoats` so a canoe left on a riverbank is still there after a restart and visible to other players.
- Carrying: pick up a set-down canoe to carry it on your head, rendered inverted over the player and visible to others; walking while carrying is **very slow** (a starting target of roughly a third to half walking speed), running, jumping, vehicles, fishing, gathering and building are blocked until it is set down, and the carried canoe counts toward the carry weight. Set it down anywhere on dry ground; it stays there until picked up by its owner (decide whether others can take it — the default should be owner only, with a world rule).
- Launching: stand at any shore or riverbank with the canoe and launch directly into the water; no dock is needed, so canoes are the way up shallow headwaters that no other boat can reach, subject to a minimal depth. Beach by paddling to any bank and stepping out; the canoe is left moored there. Boathouses, the pier and river docks also board and moor canoes.
- Storage: carry it into your home to store it (it stops existing in the world and appears in the home inventory); take it out again at the door. Decide a sensible per-home limit.

- Tests: each state transition, carry speed and blocked actions, launching from a bank and a shoreline, upstream reach past the canal boat limit, mooring persistence across restart, home storage, two players and ownership, and rendering of the inverted carried canoe.

## Planned — Storm capsizing

- Add a **capsize risk** for boats caught in a storm, driven by the existing weather and sea state (`storm` flag and `waveAmplitude(wind, storm)` in `src/shared/ocean.ts`, the marine visibility and forecast). The server evaluates risk per afloat vessel at a steady cadence from a deterministic seeded random source so tests can reproduce it; nothing capsizes in calm or merely rough seas.
- **Small craft (canoe, rowboat, lifeboat):** anywhere on the bay or open sea, a storm gives a meaningful per-minute probability of capsizing that rises with wave amplitude and distance from shore; sheltered rivers and ponds are exempt. Capsizing is death for the occupants under the ordinary death rules — respawn like any other death — with the boat lost or recoverable per the world's boat death rules.
- **Motorboat and canal boat:** safe near the coast and on rivers, but in **very deep true ocean** during a **large storm** there is a real chance of capsizing. Define "true ocean" from the existing soundings (`waterDepth`, the 8 m deep-fishing depth and 150 m shore distance are the current offshore markers; the threshold for this rule should be much further out and deeper, for example beyond 1 km from any coast and over 25 m of water, with final numbers from a survey of the bay and the new coasts), and "large storm" from wave amplitude above a configurable threshold. Capsizing kills the occupants and **loses the boat and its cargo regardless of the recovery setting**, since the hull is gone.
- **Seaworthy hulls (trawler, charter boat, tug, cargo boat):** exempt or with a far lower risk only in the most extreme seas; they are the vessels meant to be at sea in weather. Make all thresholds and multipliers world rules alongside the existing **Boats and death** settings, with public Puddlewick defaults chosen to make storms dangerous but avoidable.
- Fairness: the storm must be visible and foreseeable — rising seas, the forecast, falling visibility, spray and a warning notice when a vessel crosses into the risk zone during or ahead of a storm — and the risk should ramp over minutes so a captain who turns back lives. Crew and passengers aboard die with the captain. Log the loss with a notice naming the vessel, position and sea state.
- Tests: no risk in calm seas or on rivers, small-craft risk in the bay, motorboat/canal-boat risk only beyond the deep-ocean threshold in a large storm, exemption for seaworthy hulls, deterministic outcomes from a seed, death and boat-loss consequences per setting, crew deaths, warning timing, and that the ramp gives time to reach shelter at each hull's speed.
